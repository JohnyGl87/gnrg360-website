Engineering project management and supervision for plants and infrastructure

I manage and supervise engineering projects in plants and infrastructure, on the client's behalf, from scope definition to commissioning. I am a mechanical engineer with an MBA and a former plant manager at ICL.

I can join at the start of a project or at a later stage.

Book an introductory call ↘

What the service covers

  1. 01

    Scope and design

    • Defining the need and the project goals with the client.
    • Writing the technical specification and requirements.
    • Working with designers and consultants, and checking the design against needs on the ground.
  2. 02

    Budget and schedule

    • Cost estimates and budget.
    • A schedule that accounts for plant operation, shutdowns and delivery times.
    • Reviewing supplier and contractor proposals.
  3. 03

    Execution supervision

    • Supervising contractors on site: quality, safety and conformity with the design.
    • Ongoing tracking of progress, budget and changes.
    • Regular reporting to the client and management.
  4. 04

    Handover and commissioning

    • Acceptance tests and handover.
    • Supporting commissioning until the system runs properly.
    • Documentation for operation and maintenance.
  5. 05

    Turnkey/EPC projects

    In a turnkey/EPC project, one main contractor is responsible for engineering, procurement and construction. I can manage and supervise such a project on the client's behalf, according to the engagement scope.

The exact deliverables are agreed in writing at the start.

A project inside a running plant

  • Coordination with production. Work that needs a shutdown is planned ahead, in time windows agreed with management.
  • Safety. Contractors working inside a plant need to know its hazards and site rules.
  • Interface with maintenance. The new system has to fit what the maintenance team can support.
  • Documentation. At the end, the plant team receives every document needed for operation.

How we work

  1. Introductory call. We look at the project, its stage and what is needed.
  2. Scope definition. We agree in writing on the role, the phases and the deliverables.
  3. Fee proposal. Based on the agreed scope.
  4. Ongoing work. Management, supervision and reporting as agreed.
  5. Close-out. Handover, commissioning and documentation.

What I bring to the project

  • Inside experience. I am a former plant manager at ICL. I know how project work affects production and maintenance.
  • Large-site project management. I have managed projects on behalf of PCI at the Intel site.
  • Engineering and economics. Mechanical engineer (B.Sc., Technion) with an MBA. Every decision is weighed both technically and economically.
  • Energy and natural gas. On projects involving energy or gas systems, I understand the technical field too. See natural gas for industry and external energy manager.

Who it suits

  • Plants planning an engineering project without an in-house project manager available.
  • Organizations that need owner's-side supervision of a main contractor.
  • Infrastructure and energy projects.

Fees

FAQ

What is the difference between project management and supervision?

They are two different roles. Project management covers planning, budget, schedule and decisions. Supervision checks that site work matches the design, the standards and the contract. You can engage both or one.

Can you join a project that is already under way?

Yes. We start by reviewing where the project stands, then agree on the role from that point.

What is a turnkey/EPC project?

Three areas under one contractor: engineering, procurement and construction. The scope is set in the contract.

Who carries out the work on site?

The construction contractors. My role is management and supervision on the client's behalf.
