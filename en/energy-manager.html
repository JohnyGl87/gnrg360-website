External energy manager for industrial plants

I serve as an external energy manager for plants using 300 metric tons of fuel-oil equivalent a year or more. I am a mechanical engineer and a certified energy manager (Ministry of Energy).

The service helps your site meet the regulatory requirements. It also gives you a clear picture of where your energy goes and what is worth improving.

Book an introductory call ↘

Who must appoint an energy manager?

Under the Energy Sources (Supervision of Energy-Consumption Efficiency) Regulations, 1993, the duty applies to every "consuming plant":

  • a plant that uses at least 300 metric tons of fuel-oil equivalent a year, in fuels or electricity;
  • any statutory corporation (established by or under a law), whatever its consumption.

Electricity counts at 260 g per kWh. An all-electric site reaches the threshold at about 1.15 GWh a year.

The same plants must file an annual energy-consumption report with the Ministry of Energy by March 31.

What the regulations require of the energy manager

The main duties set by the regulations:

  • fix energy-wasting faults quickly, including steam, water and compressed-air leaks;
  • prevent energy supply where, when or at an intensity it is not needed;
  • supervise operation and maintenance for maximum efficiency;
  • measure electricity and fuel use every month, and energy per unit of product;
  • keep metering in working order, and record and keep the readings;
  • make sure periodic efficiency tests are carried out and recorded;
  • recommend energy-saving measures and help implement them;
  • train staff and prepare energy-saving instructions.

According to the Ministry, the energy manager is also responsible for the annual consumption report. Above the survey threshold, the energy manager is responsible for making sure the survey is done.

What the service includes

  • Monthly tracking of electricity, natural gas and fuel bills.
  • Preparing and filing the annual consumption report in the Ministry's online system.
  • Staff training and written energy-saving procedures.
  • An annual summary for management: consumption, trends and recommendations, each with a feasibility calculation.
  • Site visits as needed, including on-site measurements.

For measurements I use a power analyzer, a thermal camera and an ultrasonic compressed-air leak detector.

How we start

  1. Introductory call. We look at the site, its energy sources and the requirements that apply.
  2. Data collection. Energy bills, a list of the main systems and any previous reports.
  3. Site walk-through. I get to know the systems and how they are operated.
  4. Initial baseline assessment, within about a month. A consumption map, the requirements that apply, and what to check first.
  5. Ongoing work. Monthly tracking, the annual report to the Ministry, training and the annual summary for management.

ISO 50001 energy management systems

ISO 50001 is the international standard for energy management systems. It organizes what an energy manager does anyway: measurement, targets, responsibilities and continual improvement.

It also matters for regulation. Sites that must carry out a mandatory energy survey submit a new survey plan every 4.5 years. The cycle becomes 6 years on one condition: a valid ISO 50001 certification in each of the three years before the due date. The certification must come from a certification body accredited under IAF.

When I support an implementation, I:

  • write the energy-management procedures;
  • train the team;
  • run an internal audit before the certification audit;
  • support the organization during the certification audit.

A typical implementation takes 6 to 12 months, depending on the size of the organization and the state of its data.

Why an external energy manager

The regulations require the energy manager to be qualified: a certificate from a Ministry-approved energy-manager course, or a pass in the Ministry's exam. They do not require the energy manager to be an employee of the plant.

An external energy manager suits a plant with no qualified employee, or no one with time for the role.

What I bring to the role:

  • Operations experience. I am a former plant manager at ICL. I understand operations and maintenance from the plant manager's side.
  • Engineering and economics. Mechanical engineer (B.Sc., Technion) with an MBA. Every recommendation comes with a feasibility calculation.
  • Energy and natural gas. For plants that use natural gas, I know the gas system too. See natural gas for industry.
  • Close knowledge of the requirements. I am an approved lecturer in the Ministry of Energy's energy-manager course at Mishlav College.

Penalties

Failing to appoint a qualified energy manager or to file the annual report can lead to an administrative fine. A continuing violation adds a monthly surcharge.

Fees

FAQ

Does the energy manager have to be an employee?

No. The regulations require a qualified energy manager. They do not require that person to be an employee.

When is the annual consumption report due?

March 31 of the following year. The 2026 report is due by March 31, 2027, unless an extension is published.

How do I know if my plant reaches the 300-ton threshold?

Add up all fuels and electricity, counting each kWh as 260 g.

Does the service include carrying out an energy survey?

No. The mandatory energy survey is a separate obligation for consumers above 1,250 metric tons of oil equivalent a year. Above that threshold, the energy manager is responsible for making sure the survey is done on time.

How soon do I get an initial baseline assessment?

About one month after we start.
